Tracking

First-click vs last-click attribution and why server-side tracking matters

Sofiat Adedeji Updated 22 Aug 20266 min read
Quick answer

Attribution models decide which channel gets credit for a lead: first-click credits discovery, last-click credits the final touch. Browser privacy changes mean many conversions are no longer tracked, so server-side tracking sends events directly from your server to restore accuracy.

On this page

  1. Attribution models in plain English
  2. Why your numbers don't match
  3. What server-side tracking fixes
  4. Bring it back to the CRM
  5. FAQ

Attribution models in plain English

  • First-click: credits the channel that introduced the customer. Good for valuing awareness.
  • Last-click: credits the final touch before converting. Good for valuing closing channels.
  • Multi-touch: spreads credit across the journey.

Why your numbers don't match

Each ad platform tends to claim credit for conversions it touched, using its own lookback window. Your CRM sees something different. Without a single source of truth, budgets end up following whichever dashboard looks best.

What server-side tracking fixes

Ad blockers and privacy features stop many browser pixels from firing. Server-side tracking sends conversion events from your server or platform directly to Meta, Google and others, so fewer conversions are lost. This GoHighLevel attribution setup combined three pixels with server-side tracking and a 30-day lookback.

Bring it back to the CRM

Store the original source on every contact. When you can see which channel produced actual customers, not just leads, marketing decisions get much easier.

FAQ

Which attribution model should I use?

Many small businesses start with first-click for lead source reporting and look at last-click for campaign optimisation. Consistency matters more than perfection.

Is server-side tracking complicated to set up?

Platforms like GoHighLevel and Shopify now make it much easier, but it still needs careful testing to avoid double-counting.
