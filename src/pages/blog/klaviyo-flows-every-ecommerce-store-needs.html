Why flows beat campaigns for steady revenue

Campaigns are what most stores think of as "email marketing": a newsletter or promotion sent to everyone at once. They're useful, but they depend on someone sitting down to write and send them. Flows are different. They're triggered by what a shopper actually does, so the message arrives exactly when the person is most interested.

That's why a well-built flow set tends to become a store's most dependable email revenue: it runs every day, for every visitor, without anyone pressing send. The six flows below are the foundation I build first in almost every Klaviyo account.

1. Welcome series

Trigger: someone joins your newsletter list (usually from a signup form or pop-up).

The welcome series is your first impression, and new subscribers open these emails more than almost anything else you send. Use it to deliver any promised discount, tell your brand story and show social proof.

  • Email 1 (immediately): deliver the offer, set expectations and show best-sellers.
  • Email 2 (day 2): your story: why the brand exists and what makes it different.
  • Email 3 (day 4): reviews, user-generated content or a "how to choose" guide.
  • Email 4 (day 6): last-chance reminder for the welcome offer.

Add a flow filter or conditional split for "Placed Order zero times since starting this flow", so buyers exit immediately instead of receiving discount reminders after they've already purchased.

2. Abandoned checkout (abandoned cart)

Trigger: Started Checkout (Shopify) or Added to Cart, if your setup tracks it.

This flow reaches shoppers who were seconds away from buying. Keep it simple and helpful rather than pushy.

  • Email 1 (2–4 hours): a friendly reminder with a dynamic block showing the exact items left behind.
  • Email 2 (24 hours): answer objections: shipping, returns, sizing, reviews.
  • Email 3 (48–72 hours, optional): a small incentive, ideally only for higher-value carts or first-time customers.
Blueprint tipSplit by customer type. Returning customers rarely need a discount to finish checking out; new visitors sometimes do. Protect your margins by only offering incentives where they change behaviour.

3. Browse abandonment

Trigger: Viewed Product, for identified profiles only.

Browse abandonment catches people earlier in the journey, while they're still researching. Show the product they viewed, two or three related items and a reason to come back, such as reviews, a fit guide or limited stock. Add a filter to exclude anyone who has started checkout or ordered recently, so the flows don't overlap.

4. Post-purchase

Trigger: Placed Order.

The sale isn't the finish line; it's the start of the relationship. A strong post-purchase flow reduces refund requests, increases reviews and sets up the second order.

  • Thank you and what to expect (immediately after order): reassurance and delivery information.
  • How to get the most from your product (on delivery): care tips, tutorials, FAQs.
  • Review request (a week or two after delivery).
  • Cross-sell or replenishment, timed to when customers usually reorder.

Use a conditional split for first-time versus repeat buyers. A first order deserves a warmer, more educational welcome to the brand.

5. Win-back

Trigger: Placed Order, with a long time delay (for example 60–120 days, depending on your typical repurchase cycle) and a filter that the person hasn't ordered since.

Win-back emails re-engage customers who are drifting away. Start with "here's what's new", follow with a personalised recommendation, and save your strongest offer for the final email. Anyone who re-engages moves back into your regular campaigns.

6. Sunset (list cleaning)

Trigger: added to a segment of profiles who haven't opened or clicked in a long period (often 90–180 days).

The sunset flow protects your deliverability. Send one or two "Do you still want to hear from us?" emails. Anyone who doesn't engage is suppressed, which keeps your engagement rates healthy and stops mailbox providers from treating your emails as unwanted. It also lowers your Klaviyo bill, which is based on profile count.

Common mistakes that quietly cost revenue

  1. No exit conditions. Buyers keep receiving "you forgot something" emails after they've paid.
  2. Overlapping flows. One shopper gets browse, cart and welcome emails on the same day. Use flow filters and smart sending.
  3. Untested dynamic blocks. Product images or prices break for certain products. Always preview with real test profiles.
  4. Discounting everyone. Train your audience to wait for coupons and you'll pay for it on every order.
  5. Ignoring authentication. Without SPF, DKIM and DMARC on a branded sending domain, even perfect flows can land in spam. See the deliverability checklist.

FAQ

Which Klaviyo flow should I set up first?

Start with the welcome series and the abandoned checkout flow. They reach people at their moments of highest intent, so they usually produce revenue fastest.

How many emails should an abandoned cart flow have?

Two to three is a good default: a reminder within a few hours, an objection-handling follow-up the next day, and an optional final nudge for higher-value carts.

What's the difference between a flow and a campaign?

A campaign is a one-off send at a chosen time. A flow is an automated series triggered by behaviour, such as subscribing, starting checkout or buying, and it runs continuously.
